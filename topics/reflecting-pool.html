The Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool case

Evidence Strength Moderate
Velocity Rising
Clusters 2

A day after a federal indictment, this is what the page would have shown — before anyone knew how it would end.

Damage to the Reflecting Pool, discovered after a $14 million renovation, led to a federal indictment of former Olympic canoeist David Hearn on July 2 for deliberate destruction of government property. Hearn has publicly denied it, saying he only touched the water out of curiosity.

At this point, the claim is real and legally weighted — but contested and unresolved. An indictment is strong evidence someone believes they can prove the case. It is not proof. Confidence reflects that: elevated, not certain.

On July 31, prosecutors moved to dismiss the case entirely. New documents from the Interior Department indicated the damage was actually caused by a botched installation from the renovation contractor — not vandalism at all. Hearn had maintained from the start that he simply touched the water out of curiosity.

This is the case for why this page exists. The indictment got weeks of national coverage. The dismissal, which reverses the entire premise of the story, is getting a fraction of that attention and will likely be forgotten within days. Most people who remember this story will remember it wrong. The "as of Jul 3" view above is exactly what a reader would have seen if they'd checked this page back then — moderate confidence, real evidence, genuinely uncertain outcome. Nobody needed to have predicted the ending. The page just needed to stay honest about what wasn't known yet.
