Two Kinds of Plotlines

  • Living Topics Ongoing narratives, trends, or debates that don't have a fixed end. These evolve over time as new evidence emerges — we preserve the trajectory rather than forcing a premature conclusion.
  • Bounded Claims Specific assertions with an eventual outcome — a price, a date, a charge — that resolve as Confirmed, Disproven, or Partial. We track evidence until a conclusion is actually reached.

Both are tracked the same way: evidence accumulates, Evidence Strength updates, and Velocity shows which way the plotlines — the sources — currently point. Nothing is treated as resolved until it actually is.

How We Score a Topic

  • Evidence Strength How closely the accumulated evidence still lines up with the premise a topic started with — Low, Moderate, High, or Collapsed. This isn't a belief we're asserting: it's computed directly from each piece of evidence's tier and direction, plotted against when that evidence actually came out. We're not telling you how much to believe something — we're reporting how the evidence has moved.
  • Velocity Which way the evidence is currently pointing — Rising, Falling, or Steady. Applies the same way to Living Topics and Bounded Claims alike.
  • Clusters The number of independent lines of evidence behind the current Evidence Strength — not just repeated mentions of the same source.
  • Tier How directly a piece of evidence bears on the claim. Tier 1 is a primary source — a filing, a direct statement, a government record. Tier 2 is reporting or secondary analysis. Tier 3 is commentary, opinion, or unverified reports — social posts, punditry, anything not yet independently confirmed — weighted lowest of the three.

How a Bounded Claim Resolves

  • Confirmed The claim held up as stated.
  • Disproven The claim did not hold up.
  • Partial Part of the claim held. Part of it didn't.

Every bounded claim gets one of these, in public, whether or not anyone is still paying attention by the time it resolves. But a resolution isn't necessarily permanent: if new evidence surfaces afterward, we reopen the claim — status returns to Ongoing, the prior outcome is cleared, and the evidence trajectory picks up from where it left off. Nothing stays settled just because it was settled once.

What This Isn't

  • Not a prediction market We don't set odds or take positions on outcomes we can't yet see.
  • Not advice We don't tell you what to buy, vote for, or believe.
  • Not breaking news If something happened five minutes ago, this probably isn't the place for it yet.
