About

Nutgraph preserves the essential narrative of a story as it changes over time.

In journalism, the nut graph is the part of a story that tells you why it matters. It gives the facts around it a center of gravity.

Nutgraph applies that idea to a claim's life. We track evidence as it accumulates, preserving the developments that moved the story forward and the sources behind them, until the claim actually resolves.

How We Value a Topic

  • Evidence Strength How closely the accumulated evidence still lines up with the claim as stated — Low, Moderate, High, or Collapsed. This isn't a belief we're asserting: it's computed directly from each piece of evidence's tier and direction, plotted against when that evidence actually came out. We're not telling you how much to believe something — we're reporting how the evidence has moved.
  • Narrative Direction Which way the evidence is currently pointing — Rising, Falling, or Steady.
  • Clusters The number of independent lines of evidence behind the current Evidence Strength — not just repeated mentions of the same source.
  • Tier How directly a piece of evidence bears on the claim. Tier 1 is a primary source — a filing, a direct statement, a government record. Tier 2 is reporting or secondary analysis. Tier 3 is commentary, opinion, or unverified reports — social posts, punditry, anything not yet independently confirmed — weighted lowest of the three.

How a Claim Resolves

  • Confirmed The claim held up as stated.
  • Disproven The claim did not hold up.
  • Partial Part of the claim held. Part of it didn't.

*If new evidence on a topic surfaces, we reopen the claim and the status returns to Ongoing, the prior outcome is cleared, and the evidence trajectory picks up from where it left off.
