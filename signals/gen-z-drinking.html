Gen Z isn't drinking less — they're drinking differently

Premise Strength Moderate
Velocity Rising
Clusters 3

Two headlines can't both be true: one says young people have quit drinking, the other says alcohol brands are giving up on them and chasing Gen X spend instead. The evidence suggests both are measuring something real — just not the same thing.

Participation is genuinely down. CDC survey data shows the share of 18-to-24-year-olds who drink at all fell 8 points between 2011 and 2023, and the share of Gen Z reporting zero drinking in the past year rose from 17% to 24% in a single year, per Attest's 2026 research.

But among those who still drink, spend is not shrinking — it's shifting. IWSR data shows the share of Gen Z reporting a drink in the last six months actually rose from 46% to 70% between 2023 and 2025, and Bacardi's 2026 trend research found their spend now matches the average across all age cohorts. Purchases of ready-to-drink cocktails jumped from 31% to 42% in a year.

The likely read: a shrinking population of Gen Z drinkers is spending roughly the same per person, concentrated in premium, single-serve, RTD formats — consistent with brands narrowing focus to reliable spenders while the "kids don't drink anymore" narrative is also true at the population level. Not a contradiction. Two different denominators.
